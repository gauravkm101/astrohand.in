How to Read a Kundli: A Beginner’s Guide to Your Birth Chart

8 min read · By AstroHand Editorial Team · Updated 2026-07-24

The first time most people see their kundli — the Vedic birth chart — they feel a small wave of confusion. A diamond-shaped diagram, twelve compartments, a scattering of abbreviations and numbers. It looks less like a portrait of a life and more like a geometry puzzle. Yet once you learn the handful of rules that organise it, the kundli becomes surprisingly readable. This guide walks you through that first reading, step by step.

Step One: Understand the Frame

A North Indian kundli is a fixed diamond divided into twelve triangular and square compartments. The crucial thing to grasp is that in this style, the houses are fixed and the signs move. The top-centre diamond is always the first house, and the houses proceed anticlockwise from there. This is the reverse of the South Indian style, where the signs are fixed and you locate the first house by finding the Lagna.

Every kundli therefore begins with one question: which sign sits in that first house?

Step Two: Find Your Lagna (Ascendant)

The Lagna, or Ascendant, is the sign of the zodiac that was rising on the eastern horizon at the exact moment and place of your birth. It changes roughly every two hours, which is why an accurate birth time matters so much — two people born on the same day but a few hours apart can have entirely different Lagnas and, as a result, entirely different charts.

The Lagna is written in the first house, usually as a number from 1 to 12 (1 for Aries, 2 for Taurus, and so on) or by name. It sets the reference point for everything else. Your Lagna describes your outward personality, your body, your temperament, and the lens through which you meet the world. A person with Aries Lagna leads with drive and directness; a Libra Lagna leads with charm and a hunger for balance.

Step Three: Place the Twelve Signs

Once the Lagna sign is fixed in the first house, the remaining eleven signs fill the houses in their natural order. If your Lagna is Gemini (sign 3), then house one holds Gemini, house two holds Cancer, house three holds Leo, and so on around the diamond. In a computer-generated chart like AstroHand's, this is done for you — but knowing the logic lets you sanity-check what you see.

Step Four: Read the Houses

Each of the twelve houses (bhavas) governs a department of life. A working memory of them unlocks most of a reading:

1st — Self: body, personality, vitality, overall direction.
2nd — Wealth & speech: money, family, food, what you value.
3rd — Courage & siblings: effort, communication, younger siblings, skills.
4th — Home & heart: mother, property, inner peace, education.
5th — Creativity & children: intelligence, romance, children, past-life merit.
6th — Struggles & service: enemies, debts, illness, daily work, discipline.
7th — Partnership: marriage, spouse, business partners, the public.
8th — Transformation: longevity, sudden events, inheritance, the hidden.
9th — Fortune & dharma: luck, father, guru, higher learning, long journeys.
10th — Career & status: profession, reputation, karma in the world.
11th — Gains & networks: income, friends, elder siblings, fulfilled desires.
12th — Loss & liberation: expenses, foreign lands, sleep, spirituality, letting go.

When you read a chart, you are essentially asking: which planets sit in each of these rooms, and what does that suggest about that area of life?

Step Five: Read the Planets

Now place the grahas (planets) into the houses. Each planet carries a core nature: the Sun is authority and soul; the Moon is mind and emotion; Mars is energy and courage; Mercury is intellect and speech; Jupiter is wisdom and expansion; Venus is love, beauty, and comfort; Saturn is discipline, delay, and endurance; Rahu is ambition and obsession; Ketu is detachment and past-life mastery.

A planet colours the house it sits in. Jupiter in the 5th, for example, blesses intelligence and children; Saturn in the 7th can delay marriage but reward it with stability; Mars in the 10th drives a forceful, action-oriented career. You are learning to read sentences of the form "*this energy, expressing in this area of life*."

Step Six: Consider Sign, Strength, and Aspect

Beyond mere placement, three refinements deepen the reading. First, the sign a planet occupies — a planet in its own sign or exaltation is strong and gives fuller results; in a difficult sign it struggles. Second, planetary aspects (drishti) — planets "look at" other houses (all planets aspect the 7th house from themselves; Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn have additional special aspects), spreading their influence across the chart. Third, the running dasha — the planetary period currently active, which determines whose promises are being delivered right now.

Putting It Together

A first reading does not require mastery of all of this at once. Start simply: find your Lagna, note your Moon sign and Nakshatra, see which planets sit in the 1st, 7th, and 10th houses, and read those as clues about your personality, relationships, and career. That alone tells a coherent little story.

A word of honesty that the tradition itself insists on: the Lagna in most quick tools, including simplified engines, uses an equal-house approximation, and precise predictive work needs an exact birth time and a trained eye. A birth chart reveals tendencies and timing, not a fixed fate. Read it as a mirror and a map — a way to understand yourself more clearly and to act with better timing — and it will serve you well.
