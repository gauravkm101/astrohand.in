Manglik Dosha: What It Is and Common Remedies

6 min read · AstroHand Editorial

Few terms in Vedic astrology cause as much worry as "Manglik Dosha." For many families, especially around the time of marriage, it can be a source of real anxiety. But when understood calmly and in context, Manglik Dosha is far less frightening than its reputation suggests. This article explains what it is, why it matters, and the traditional remedies associated with it.

What Is Manglik Dosha?

Manglik Dosha, also called Mangal Dosha or Kuja Dosha, is an astrological condition said to occur when the planet Mars (Mangal) is placed in certain houses of a person’s birth chart — traditionally the first, second, fourth, seventh, eighth, or twelfth house from the Ascendant. A person with this placement is called a Manglik.

Mars is a fiery, energetic planet associated with passion, drive, and aggression. The concern in these particular house placements is that Mars’s intense energy may create friction in marriage and partnership matters, which is why the dosha is most discussed in the context of marriage compatibility.

Keeping It in Perspective

Here is the balanced truth that experienced astrologers emphasize: Manglik Dosha is very common, and its effects are often mild or fully neutralized by other factors in the chart. A single placement rarely tells the whole story.

There are several important nuances. The dosha can be cancelled or reduced by many chart combinations — this is called Manglik Dosha Bhanga (cancellation). If both partners are Manglik, the effect is traditionally considered to cancel out. The strength and sign of Mars matters greatly; a well-placed Mars behaves very differently from a poorly-placed one. And Mars often naturally reduces in influence as a person grows older.

In short, being Manglik is not a curse, and it certainly does not doom a marriage. It is one factor among many, and it deserves a measured, informed look rather than fear.

Traditional Remedies

For those who wish to follow tradition, several remedies are commonly recommended. These are cultural and devotional practices, offered here for information rather than as guarantees.

Common remedies include reciting the Hanuman Chalisa and worshipping Lord Hanuman, who is associated with Mars. Fasting on Tuesdays, the day ruled by Mars, is another traditional practice. Some families perform specific pujas before marriage. The Kumbh Vivah, a symbolic ritual, is sometimes recommended in stronger cases. Wearing a red coral (Moonga) gemstone is also traditionally associated with balancing Mars, though gemstones should only be worn after proper astrological consultation.

A Balanced Final Word

If you or a loved one is told they are Manglik, the best response is calm and information, not panic. Consult a knowledgeable astrologer who can look at the complete chart, including whether the dosha is cancelled and how strong Mars actually is. Marriage success depends far more on understanding, effort, communication, and compatibility of values than on any single astrological factor.
