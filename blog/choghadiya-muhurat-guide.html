Choghadiya and Muhurat: How to Choose an Auspicious Time the Vedic Way

7 min read · By AstroHand Editorial Team · Updated 2026-07-22

Across India, before a journey, a purchase, a shop opening, or a new venture, someone will pause and ask: *is this a good time?* Behind that everyday question lies one of the most practical branches of Vedic astrology — the art of muhurat, choosing an auspicious moment, and its most accessible tool, Choghadiya. Unlike the deep chart analysis that other Jyotish requires, Choghadiya is simple enough to use daily, which is exactly why it has survived in ordinary life for centuries.

What Choghadiya Means

The word Choghadiya comes from *chas* (four) and *ghadi* (a traditional unit of time of about 24 minutes) — so a Choghadiya is roughly a ninety-six-minute block, though in practice each slot is one-eighth of the day or night. The system divides both the daytime and the nighttime into eight slots each, and assigns to every slot a quality: favourable, neutral, or to be avoided. To time an activity well, you simply choose a favourable slot.

Built on Real Sunrise and Sunset

Here is the detail that separates a genuine Choghadiya from a fake one. The eight daytime slots are not clock hours — they run from actual sunrise to actual sunset, divided into eight equal parts. The eight night slots run from sunset to the next sunrise. Because sunrise and sunset shift with your location and the season, correct Choghadiya must be computed for your city on your date. The Seven Choghadiya and Their Meanings

Each slot is ruled by one of seven planetary energies, and its quality follows:

- Amrit (Moon) — the most auspicious; "nectar." Ideal for any important beginning.
- Shubh (Jupiter) — auspicious; excellent for ceremonies, marriage-related work, and worship.
- Labh (Mercury) — "gain"; favourable for business, trade, and learning.
- Char (Venus) — "moving"; good for travel and activity, neutral-to-positive.
- Udveg (Sun) — associated with anxiety; generally avoided for new beginnings.
- Rog (Mars) — associated with conflict and illness; avoided.
- Kaal (Saturn) — associated with obstacles; avoided for auspicious starts.

The four you want are Amrit, Shubh, Labh, and Char; the three to avoid for important beginnings are Udveg, Rog, and Kaal.

The Weekday Sequence

The order in which these slots appear is not random — it follows a fixed weekday sequence. The first daytime slot depends on the planetary ruler of the day: Sunday begins with Udveg, Monday with Amrit, Tuesday with Rog, Wednesday with Labh, Thursday with Shubh, Friday with Char, and Saturday with Kaal. From that starting slot, the seven Choghadiya cycle in order through all eight daytime slots (the eighth repeats the first), and the night follows its own starting sequence. This is why the same clock time is auspicious on one day and not on another.

Muhurat: Beyond Choghadiya

Choghadiya is the quick, daily layer of timing. For truly important events — a wedding, a house-warming (griha pravesh), the start of a major business — traditional muhurat selection goes deeper. It considers the tithi (lunar day), the nakshatra (lunar mansion) the Moon occupies, the yoga and karana of the day, and avoids inauspicious windows such as Rahu Kaal. Certain nakshatras favour certain activities; certain tithis, like the "empty" Rikta tithis (the 4th, 9th, and 14th), are traditionally avoided for auspicious beginnings.

A complete muhurat therefore layers several filters: a favourable Choghadiya slot, a suitable tithi, an appropriate nakshatra, and the avoidance of known bad windows. How to Use It Practically

For an everyday task — buying a vehicle, signing a document, beginning a journey — checking the Choghadiya is enough. Open the tool for your city and date, and pick the next Amrit, Shubh, or Labh slot; each activity has traditionally preferred slots (Labh and Amrit for business, Shubh and Amrit for ceremonies, Char and Labh for travel).

For once-in-a-lifetime events, treat Choghadiya as the first filter and then confirm the nakshatra and tithi, ideally with an astrologer, since these events deserve the fuller muhurat analysis.

A Word of Balance

Muhurat is a beautiful tradition, but it works best held lightly. Choosing an auspicious time aligns you with a supportive rhythm; it does not make effort optional, nor does a "bad" slot doom a well-prepared undertaking. Life does not always wait for Amrit, and readiness, honesty, and diligence matter more than any single window. Think of Choghadiya as a tailwind you can catch when it is available — helpful, worth using, and never a substitute for doing the work well.
