Doshas

Types of Kundli Dosh and How to Check Them Free

8 min read · By AstroHand Editorial Team · Updated 2026-07-24

The word dosha appears everywhere in astrology, and it frightens far more people than it should. A dosha is simply a specific planetary affliction or imbalance in a birth chart — a pattern that may create obstacles in one area of life. It is not a curse, not a permanent sentence, and almost every dosha comes with recognised conditions of cancellation and time-honoured remedies. Here is a clear map of the doshas people most often ask about, and how to check your own chart honestly.

Mangal Dosha (Manglik)

Formed when Mars occupies certain houses (traditionally the 1st, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th) from the ascendant, Moon or Venus, Mangal Dosha is examined mainly for marriage. Its reputation is far larger than its reality: there are many cancellation rules, and when both partners are Manglik the dosha is often considered mutually balanced.

Kaal Sarp Dosha

This forms when all seven major planets fall between Rahu and Ketu — the lunar nodes — on one side of the chart. It is associated with a sense of struggle or delayed rewards, but classical texts also describe many partial and cancelled forms, and some charts with it belong to highly accomplished people.

Sade Sati

Not a birth dosha but a transit — the roughly seven-and-a-half-year passage of Saturn over the sign before, on, and after your Moon sign. Sade Sati is a period of hard lessons and maturing, not a catastrophe, and it comes to everyone in turn.

Pitra Dosh

The "ancestral debt," read from an afflicted Sun, ninth house and the nodes. Its remedies are among the gentlest in astrology — remembrance, tarpan and service to elders rather than costly rituals.

Nadi Dosha

Chiefly relevant in marriage matching, this occurs when both partners share the same Nadi in Guna Milan, costing all eight Nadi points. Like the others, it has recognised cancellations (Nadi Dosha Parihar).

Gand Mool and others

A child born in a Gand Mool nakshatra carries Mool dosh, resolved by a simple Shanti ritual. Other patterns families ask about include Grahan dosha (Sun or Moon with Rahu/Ketu) and Shani Dhaiya (Saturn's two-and-a-half-year transit). Each has its own meaning and remedy.

Why doshas are so often exaggerated

It helps to understand *why* the word carries so much fear. Doshas are dramatic and memorable, which makes them easy to sensationalise — and unfortunately, some practitioners use that fear to sell expensive gemstones, pujas and "guaranteed" cures. The classical texts themselves are far more measured. For nearly every dosha they describe, they also describe the conditions under which it is weakened or entirely cancelled (bhang or parihar), and the remedies they prescribe are usually simple acts of discipline, charity and devotion rather than costly rituals. A dosha in your chart is an invitation to a little extra care in one area of life — not a life sentence, and never a reason to be exploited.

How to check your doshas honestly

Every one of these is read from the exact positions of the planets in your birth chart — which is precisely what a computed kundli provides. The honest way to approach doshas is:

1. Get an accurate chart from your real birth date, time and place.
2. See which doshas are actually present, and in what strength.
3. Check for cancellations before worrying — most doshas have them.
4. Choose grounded remedies and ignore anyone using fear to sell expensive "solutions."

Doshas describe areas that may need patience and care; they do not write your fate. Read them with a cool head and they become useful guidance rather than a source of anxiety.
