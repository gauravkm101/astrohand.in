Griha Pravesh Muhurat: Choosing an Auspicious Housewarming Date

7 min read · By AstroHand Editorial Team · Updated 2026-07-24

Moving into a new home is one of life's great milestones, and in the Vedic tradition it is marked by Griha Pravesh — a housewarming ceremony that welcomes positive energy and the blessings of the deities into a fresh space. Choosing an auspicious Griha Pravesh muhurat is believed to set the tone for the peace and prosperity of everyone who will live there.

The three kinds of Griha Pravesh

Tradition recognises three types, and the rules differ slightly for each:

- Apoorva: entering a brand-new house for the very first time.
- Sapoorva: re-entering an existing home after a long absence, such as travel or renovation.
- Dwandwah (or Dwandva): returning to a home that was repaired or rebuilt after damage.

Most people planning a housewarming for a new house are looking for an Apoorva muhurat, which is treated with the greatest care.

Auspicious timing

A Griha Pravesh muhurat is chosen from the Panchang much like a wedding date:

- Months: certain months are traditionally favoured — commonly Magha, Phalguna, Vaishakha and Jyeshtha — while the Chaturmas period (when deities are said to rest) and Malmas (Adhik Maas) are avoided for a first entry.
- Nakshatra: stars considered favourable include Rohini, Mrigashira, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Anuradha, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati, Chitra and Swati.
- Tithi: auspicious tithis are preferred; Rikta tithis (4, 9, 14), Amavasya and Purnima are generally avoided.
- Weekday: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday are usually preferred; Tuesday and Saturday are often avoided for entering a new home.

What to avoid

Beyond Chaturmas and Malmas, tradition advises against entering a new home during an eclipse period, on inauspicious tithis, or when the house is still incomplete. Many families ensure at least the doors, roof and kitchen are ready, since the first cooking in the new home (boiling milk until it overflows) is part of the ritual and a symbol of abundance.

The rituals of Griha Pravesh

A Griha Pravesh is as much a set of joyful customs as it is a muhurat. It typically opens with a Ganesh puja to remove obstacles, followed by a Vastu Shanti to harmonise the energies of the home and pacify any Vastu doshas in its construction, and a havan (sacred fire) for purification. The lady of the house often enters first, right foot forward, sometimes gently tipping a small vessel of rice to symbolise incoming abundance. The mangal kalash is carried in, a lamp is lit and kept burning through the day, and — as noted — milk is boiled until it overflows as a wish for prosperity that never stops flowing.

A natural moment to consider Vastu

Because the ceremony already includes a Vastu Shanti, this is the ideal time to think about the Vastu of the home itself — the direction the main entrance faces, where the kitchen and bedrooms sit, and the placement of water sources and heavy furniture. A muhurat blesses the *timing* of your entry; Vastu concerns the *space* you are entering, and the two are complementary. Attending to both — an auspicious date and a harmonious layout — is considered the most complete way to begin life in a new home, and small adjustments made at move-in are far easier than changes later.

A grounded view

The purpose of a Griha Pravesh muhurat is to begin life in a new home with intention, gratitude and the blessings of family — not to control the future. A well-chosen date is a beautiful tradition and a joyful start, but the warmth of a home ultimately comes from the people in it. Choose a muhurat that is both astrologically sound and practical for your family, and enter your new home with a light and hopeful heart.
