Rahu and Ketu: The Shadow Planets That Shape Desire and Detachment

6 min read · AstroHand Editorial

Ask an astronomer and they will tell you Rahu and Ketu do not exist — at least not as objects. They are the two points where the Moon’s tilted orbit crosses the Sun’s apparent path, the ecliptic. Yet these two mathematical points earn planetary status in Jyotish, because whenever the Sun and Moon meet near them, the lights of the sky go dark: eclipses happen only at the nodes. A system built on light takes its shadows seriously.

The Serpent Cut in Two

Mythology renders this astronomy as story. Svarbhanu, an asura, disguised himself among the gods to drink the nectar of immortality. The Sun and Moon exposed him, and Vishnu’s discus severed him — too late. The head, Rahu, and the tail, Ketu, live on, immortal and incomplete, forever chasing the two luminaries that betrayed them. Few myths encode their meaning so precisely: Rahu, a head without a body, consumes without ever feeling full; Ketu, a body without a head, acts without appetite.

Rahu: The Amplifier

In a chart, Rahu marks where hunger concentrates — the house and sign it occupies describe what this lifetime cannot stop wanting: status, foreign lands, technology, fame, intensity. Rahu’s gifts are real: obsession is also drive, and many remarkable careers sit on a well-worked Rahu. Its risks are equally real — shortcuts, illusions, and the peculiar emptiness of getting exactly what you craved. Rahu rules the unconventional and the imported; it thrives at frontiers where rules are still being written.

Ketu: The Renouncer

Ketu marks the opposite pole — what the soul has already had enough of. Its placements show talents that arrive strangely pre-formed, needing little effort, and areas of life met with unexplained indifference. Ketu’s discomforts are subtle: dissatisfaction, sudden severances, a pull toward withdrawal. Its gift is the rarest one — genuine detachment, intuition, and the capacity for moksha, liberation. Tradition calls Ketu the moksha-karaka for good reason.

Always Opposite, Always Together

The nodes sit exactly 180 degrees apart, so every chart carries a Rahu–Ketu axis — a private tug-of-war between craving and release across two houses of life. Reading this axis honestly is one of Jyotish’s most useful exercises: chase Rahu’s house consciously, honour Ketu’s house quietly, and the axis becomes an engine instead of a trap. The nodes also give their names to the dreaded and misunderstood Kaal Sarp Yoga, and their dashas — eighteen years for Rahu, seven for Ketu — are among the most eventful periods in the Vimshottari cycle.
