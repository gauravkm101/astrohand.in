Ashtakoota Guna Milan: How Vedic Astrology Scores Marriage Compatibility

8 min read · By AstroHand Editorial Team · Updated 2026-07-24

For generations, before a marriage is arranged in a traditional Indian family, two horoscopes are compared through a system called Ashtakoota Guna Milan — the matching of eight factors for a total of thirty-six points. It is the most widely used compatibility method in Vedic astrology, and its famous "out of 36" score has decided countless matches. Understanding what it actually measures — and what it does not — is essential to using it wisely rather than superstitiously.

The Idea Behind Guna Milan

Ashtakoota Guna Milan compares the birth nakshatras and Moon signs of the prospective bride and groom across eight dimensions, called kootas. Each koota measures a different aspect of compatibility — mental, physical, temperamental, and constitutional — and carries a certain number of points. Add them up, and you get a score out of 36. The higher the score, the more the traditional system considers the couple naturally compatible.

Crucially, the whole system is built on the Moon and its nakshatra, because in Jyotish the Moon governs the mind and emotions, and marriage is, above all, a meeting of two minds and emotional natures.

The Eight Kootas

Here is what each of the eight factors measures and its weight:

1. Varna (1 point) — spiritual or ego compatibility; the broad temperamental "caste" of the mind (not social caste). It checks a basic harmony of nature.

2. Vashya (2 points) — mutual attraction and control; how naturally the two are drawn to and influence each other.

3. Tara or Dina (3 points) — health and wellbeing; the compatibility of the couple's birth stars for mutual prosperity and longevity.

4. Yoni (4 points) — physical and sexual compatibility, symbolised by animal archetypes assigned to each nakshatra; measures instinctual and intimate harmony.

5. Graha Maitri (5 points) — mental and intellectual compatibility, based on the friendship between the planetary lords of the two Moon signs; one of the most important factors for a lasting bond.

6. Gana (6 points) — temperament; each nakshatra belongs to a deva (divine), manushya (human), or rakshasa (intense) gana, and this measures the meeting of the two temperaments.

7. Bhakoot (7 points) — emotional compatibility and the overall wellbeing of the union, based on the relationship between the two Moon signs; affects prosperity, health, and family.

8. Nadi (8 points) — constitutional and genetic compatibility, the highest-weighted factor, concerning health and progeny. A "Nadi dosha" (both partners sharing the same Nadi) is traditionally taken seriously.

Add the maximum points — 1+2+3+4+5+6+7+8 — and you reach 36.

What the Score Means

Interpretation of the total is broadly:

- Below 18: traditionally considered a low match; caution advised.
- 18 to 24: an acceptable, workable match.
- 24 to 31: a good match.
- 31 to 36: an excellent, highly compatible match.

Most reasonably compatible couples score somewhere in the middle-to-upper range. A very high score is favourable, but — and this is the point most often missed — the number alone never decides a marriage.

Why the Score Is a Starting Point, Not a Verdict

Ashtakoota Guna Milan is a valuable *first filter*, but taken alone it is incomplete, and treating it as a final verdict causes real harm. Several important considerations sit outside the eight kootas:

Mangal Dosha is checked separately, alongside the Guna score, and can be cancelled by many conditions (covered in our Manglik guides).

The Navamsa (D9) charts of both partners reveal the deeper texture of married life and should be compared for any serious match.

The overall strength of each chart's seventh house, Venus, and Jupiter matters more to the actual quality of married life than the raw koota count.

And above all, real compatibility — shared values, communication, mutual respect, and genuine affection — is something no chart can fully capture. Couples with modest scores build beautiful marriages through effort and love; couples with high scores can struggle if the human foundation is weak.

The Nadi and Bhakoot "Doshas"

Two factors — Nadi (8 points) and Bhakoot (7 points) — carry the most weight, and a zero score in either is sometimes called a "dosha" and treated as a serious obstacle. Here too, tradition offers cancellations and exceptions, and a low score in one high-weight koota does not automatically doom a match, especially when other factors and the wider charts are supportive. A thoughtful astrologer weighs the whole picture rather than rejecting a match on a single koota.

Using Guna Milan Wisely

The wise use of Guna Milan is as a conversation-starter and one input among many, never as a mechanical gatekeeper. It can highlight areas where two temperaments may need conscious effort, and it can offer reassurance where natural harmony is strong. But it should never override two people's genuine compatibility, nor should a number frighten a well-suited couple apart or push an ill-suited one together. The tradition itself, at its best, always deferred to the judgment of wise elders and the couple's own hearts.
